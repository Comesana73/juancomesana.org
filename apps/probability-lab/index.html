The method

As in the book: start from the dictionary, decide whether you learned E or ¬E, identify the prior and the likelihoods, then use Bayes’ theorem to compute the posterior.

The likelihoods are always about E: P(E | H) and P(E | ¬H). If you learned ¬E, use P(¬E | H) = 1 − P(E | H) and P(¬E | ¬H) = 1 − P(E | ¬H).

Probabilities can be typed as fractions (7/26), decimals (0.27) or percentages (27%).
