# Introduction to Formal Reasoning and Decision Making (PHIL 109)

Interactive tools for the course. They run in any web browser, on a laptop or a phone, with nothing to install.

- **[Logic Lab]({{ '/apps/logic-lab/' | relative_url }})**: truth tables for formulas and arguments (with a practice mode, random practice problems and a step-by-step reveal), a syntax-tree builder, and practice translating English sentences and arguments into PL.
- **[Probability Lab]({{ '/apps/probability-lab/' | relative_url }})**: stochastic truth tables (calculating probabilities and conditionalizing), Bayes' theorem word problems worked the book's way, and eikosograms: an interactive picture of how evidence moves a prior to a posterior.
- **[Decisions & Games Lab]({{ '/apps/decisions-games-lab/' | relative_url }})**: decision matrices under ignorance and under risk (dominance, maximin and leximin, minimax regret, and (conditional) expected utility), and two-player games: iterated deletion of dominated strategies, Nash equilibria, Pareto optimality and saddle points, with classic games such as the Prisoner's Dilemma. Practice modes and random practice problems throughout.
