<p class="eyebrow">PHIL 109</p>
<h1>Introduction to Formal Reasoning and Decision Making</h1>

<p class="lede" style="margin-bottom:48px">Interactive tools for the course. They run in any web browser, on a laptop or a phone, with nothing to install.</p>

<div class="ruled">
  <article class="app">
    <h2><a href="{{ '/apps/logic-lab/' | relative_url }}">Logic Lab</a></h2>
    <p>Truth tables for formulas and arguments (with a practice mode, random practice problems and a step-by-step reveal), a syntax-tree builder, and practice translating English sentences and arguments into PL.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="app">
    <h2><a href="{{ '/apps/probability-lab/' | relative_url }}">Probability Lab</a></h2>
    <p>Stochastic truth tables (calculating probabilities and conditionalizing), Bayes' theorem word problems worked the book's way, and eikosograms: an interactive picture of how evidence moves a prior to a posterior.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="app">
    <h2><a href="{{ '/apps/decisions-games-lab/' | relative_url }}">Decisions &amp; Games Lab</a></h2>
    <p>Decision matrices under ignorance and under risk (dominance, maximin and leximin, minimax regret, and (conditional) expected utility), and two-player games: iterated deletion of dominated strategies, Nash equilibria, Pareto optimality and saddle points, with classic games such as the Prisoner's Dilemma. Practice modes and random practice problems throughout.</p>
  </article>
</div>
